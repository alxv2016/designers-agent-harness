# ux-designer — Learnability, the next step obvious

What scores of 0, 2 and 4 look like on this dimension, for the `ux-designer` judge. Whether a first-time person can see the next step without being told. Its weight is in `.agents/evals/weights.json`; how it is scored is the judge's rubric, Quality (.agents/HARNESS.md §12).

**Measured proxies — none.** The judge scores this dimension on its reading alone.

**How to use it.** Find the level the work sits nearest, quote the evidence — a file and line, a screenshot, a measured value — and give 1 or 3 when it sits between two. The person's own examples, under From the person, outrank the ones written here.

## 4 — what good looks like

- The interface implies its own use — what can be pressed looks pressable, a convention is taught once and never repeated on every screen (ACCESSIBILITY §14); recognition over recall; a new feature found where the person already looks (HCI §8).

## 2 — acceptable, with a clear gap

- The next step is findable, but only by trying; a hint that appears after a mistake rather than before.

## 0 — what fails it

- The person must remember a code, a hidden gesture or a sequence nothing on screen shows.
